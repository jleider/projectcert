## SEA methodology

The California Department of Education defines an English Learner under federal statute (Title VIII, Part A, Section 8101(20) of the Every Student Succeeds Act). Per CDE, an English Learner is an individual who:

- Is aged 3 through 21 and enrolled in school
- Was not born in the U.S. or has a native language other than English (or is Native American/Alaska Native/outlying areas resident)
- "Comes from an environment where a language other than English has had a significant impact on the individual's level of English language proficiency"
- Has difficulties in English that may prevent meeting academic standards, succeeding in English-instruction classrooms, or participating fully in society

**Current EL count excludes Reclassified Fluent English Proficient (RFEP) students.** For 2024–25, an additional 909,319 RFEP students (15.7% of total K-12 enrollment) are tracked separately, bringing the "Ever-EL" population to 1,918,385.

**Snapshot methodology:** Data are collected on Census Day, which is the first Wednesday in October of the reporting year. Enrollment data come from California Longitudinal Pupil Achievement Data System (CALPADS) and are reported through DataQuest.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest of Education Statistics Table 204.20 reported California EL percentages:
- Fall 2018: 19.1%
- Fall 2019: 18.4%
- Fall 2020: 17.5%
- Fall 2021: 18.9%

**CDE-reported figures for overlapping period:**
- 2018–19 school year: 19.8% (non-charter public schools), 19.3% (overall, per CDE press release)
- 2022–23 school year: 19.01%
- 2024–25 school year: 17.4%

**Comparison conclusion: CLOSE but with methodological divergence.**

The 2018–19 CDE figure (19.8% non-charter, 19.3% overall) is slightly higher than NCES's concurrent fall 2018–19 estimate (19.1%). The differences likely reflect:

1. **Definition scope**: CDE explicitly includes students from non-U.S. birth OR home language other than English who demonstrate English proficiency gaps. NCES (EDFacts) derives from Title III reports and may weight submissions slightly differently.
2. **Denominator difference**: NCES uses CCD (Common Core of Data) total enrollment; CDE uses CALPADS enrollment as of Census Day (first Wednesday in October). These may differ slightly.
3. **RFEP treatment**: CDE now reports RFEP separately, clarifying the distinction. Historical NCES figures do not segregate reclassified students, so the overlap period's numbers may reflect slightly different cohort definitions.

The 2024–25 decline from 19.01% (2022–23) to 17.4% (2024–25) is documented by CDE but warrants verification—it may reflect post-pandemic demographic shifts or improved reclassification practices.

## Notes / caveats

1. **Definition language is federal** (ESSA Title VIII), not state-specific. CDE applies this uniformly across all public K-12 in CALPADS.
2. **RFEPs are tracked separately** as of the most recent (2024–25) reporting, reducing historical comparability with pre-2024 NCES figures that may have lumped ever-EL together.
3. **Pandemic-era anomalies**: California's EL reclassification rate dropped sharply to 7% in 2020 and remained depressed through 2021, likely reflecting assessment delays and remote learning disruptions.
4. **Total enrollment declined**: K-12 enrollment fell from ~5.85M (2022–23) to 5.81M (2024–25), a ~0.7% decrease. The EL count fell from 1.11M to 1.01M (~9% decline), suggesting both statewide enrollment loss and improved reclassification.
5. **No direct NCES equivalence post-2021**: NCES Digest 204.20 publishes through fall 2021. CDE's most recent figures (2024–25) cannot be directly reconciled with NCES without access to parallel EDFacts submissions for 2023–2024.
