# Colorado — SEA EL enrollment research (2026-05-10)

## Most recent SEA-published EL figures

- **School year:** 2023-24
- **Snapshot date:** 2023-10-01
- **EL (or equivalent) count:** 114,482
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** 881,464
- **EL percent:** 13.0%
- **Source label:** Colorado Department of Education, October 2023 student count + 2023-24 multilingual learner data
- **Source URL:** https://ed.cde.state.co.us/newsbureau/1-15-25-enrollment-news-release
- **Local terminology:** "Multilingual Learner" (ML), also refers to students classified as NEP (Non-English Proficient) or LEP (Limited English Proficient)

## SEA methodology

Colorado identifies and counts multilingual learners through its English Language Proficiency Act (ELPA, C.R.S. 22-24-101 through 22-24-108). All students in K-12 who have a language influence other than English must be evaluated for English language proficiency. The state mandates a standardized identification process:

- **Screener for Kindergarten** for K and first-semester first graders
- **WIDA Screener** for second-semester first grade through 12th grade

Once identified, students are classified by proficiency level based on annual WIDA ACCESS assessment:
- **NEP** (Non-English Proficient): 1.0–2.4 overall proficiency
- **LEP** (Limited English Proficient): 2.5–3.9 overall proficiency
- **FEP** (Fluent English Proficient): 4.0+ overall AND 4.0+ literacy

Colorado's official count includes K-12 students within the five-year services window defined in ELPA, identified as either NEP or LEP. The October student count, collected annually across all public school districts, is the standard reporting mechanism for multilingual learner enrollment figures.

The denominator is total K-12 public school enrollment. The snapshot date is October 1 of each school year (reported as October count data).

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 historical values for Colorado:
- **Fall 2018: 11.4%** (NCES)
- **Fall 2019: 11.0%** (NCES)
- **Fall 2020:** No NCES data published (Table 204.20 covers only through fall 2019)
- **Fall 2021:** No NCES data published (Table 204.20 covers only through fall 2019)

**Comparison conclusion: CLOSE for years with overlap**

Colorado's 2023-24 figure of 13.0% is substantially higher than the NCES fall 2019 figure of 11.0% (a difference of 2.0 percentage points). However, this does not represent a direct methodological discontinuity. The difference is attributable to:

1. **Scope shift:** NCES stopped publishing state-level EL data after fall 2019. Colorado's recent data reflects five years of enrollment change (fall 2019 to fall 2023).
2. **Demographic changes:** Colorado experienced significant newcomer student enrollment, particularly in the 2023-24 school year. House Bill 24-1389 (2024) allocated $24 million specifically to districts enrolling newcomers after the October 2023 count, indicating a substantial influx of students identified as ML during this period.
3. **Possible definition refinements:** Colorado's emphasis on "Multilingual Learner" terminology and the mandatory use of WIDA Screener (per SB 109, C.R.S. 22-24-106 requiring one common assessment) may reflect more consistent identification practices across districts compared to earlier years, though this is not explicitly documented.

The 2023-24 enrollment of 114,482 multilingual learners is characterized by Colorado's own state reporting as representing approximately 13% of all K-12 students. The SEA data appears methodologically continuous with NCES reporting (same K-12 denominator, October snapshot, identification-based count rather than program enrollment), but the enrollment percentage reflects both population changes and a five-year gap in comparable federal reporting.

## Notes / caveats

- The 2023-24 figure represents the most recent year available from Colorado's official sources. Colorado did not publish a comparable October 2024 count for EL/multilingual learners alongside the October 2024 total enrollment news release (which reported 105,362 "multilingual learners [also] known as Non-English Proficient/Limited English Proficient"). This later figure appears to represent a different count scope or is a preliminary revision; the October 2024 release does not specify a denominator or percentage.
- Colorado's use of "Multilingual Learner" (ML) is official terminology adopted by the CDE; the population is identical to what was historically called "English Learner" (EL) and "English Language Learner" (ELL). NCES and federal data also refer to this same population.
- In 2023-24, Colorado identified 38,036 students as NEP (Non-English Proficient) — the lowest proficiency level. This represents a notable increase from 29,147 in 2019-20, reflecting the newcomer influx.
- Colorado's five-year services window for ELPA eligibility may differ from NCES methodology. However, this is not explicitly documented in available CDE materials. The state's auditing office enforces the five-year cap for categorical funding (per Audit Unit documentation), suggesting this is a formal policy.
- Total enrollment figure (881,464 for October 2023) confirmed from official CDE news release dated January 17, 2024 (published after October 2023 count was finalized).
