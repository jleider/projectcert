## SEA methodology

Connecticut defines English Learners (also called Multilingual Learners, EL/ML) as students whose dominant language is not English and who are not proficient enough in English to ensure equal opportunity in the regular school program (Connecticut General Statutes § 10-17 et seq.).

**Identification process:** Connecticut uses a two-step identification system. First, all new K-12 students are screened for dominant language via home language survey. All students in grades 1-12 whose home language survey identifies a language other than English are then administered the LAS Links Placement Test 2nd Edition (English version). Any student scoring "Not Proficient" or "Approaching Proficient" is identified as EL/ML.

**Denominator:** K-12 public school enrollment only (excludes pre-K and adult education).

**Snapshot date:** October (fall census date, consistent with NCES timing).

Connecticut's methodology aligns with NCES's Title III/EDFacts definitions, though Connecticut does not explicitly state in available documentation whether its count includes or excludes students in a monitoring period post-reclassification. NCES does not count such students; if Connecticut includes them, that would represent a methodological divergence.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 reports the following for Connecticut:

- Fall 2018: 7.6%
- Fall 2019: 8.0%
- Fall 2020: 7.7%
- Fall 2021: 9.4%

**Finding: NO OVERLAP.** Connecticut's publicly available SEA-published EL enrollment percentages do not extend back to the 2018-2021 window. The most recent comparable historical data located in Connecticut's official sources (Condition of Education reports) begins with 2022-23 (approximately 9.5-9.6%, derived from the statement that 2023-24 at 10.5% represented "an increase of nearly a full percentage point"). 

The 2023-24 observation (10.5%, 54,078 students) sits approximately 1.1 percentage points above the 2021 NCES figure (9.4%), suggesting a continued upward trend. However, without SEA-published data directly overlapping the 2018-2021 period, I cannot confirm whether the SEA's methodology remained constant across that interval or whether the divergence reflects genuine enrollment growth versus methodological shifts (e.g., inclusion/exclusion of monitoring-period students, timing of snapshots, or reclassification criteria).

**Conclusion: NO OVERLAP** — Connecticut's SEA does not publish historical data for fall 2018-2021. The earliest contemporary observation is 2023-24 (10.5%).

## Notes / caveats

1. **Data provenance:** All current-year figures come from Connecticut's official "Condition of Education" series published by the Connecticut State Department of Education board. These are the authoritative SEA-published figures.

2. **Enrollment decline in fall 2025:** A December 2025 news report noted that Connecticut public schools experienced their first EL enrollment decline in over a decade, with more than 2,000 fewer EL students in October 2025 compared to 2024-25. The report attributed this to immigration-related fears affecting enrollment. The 2025-26 data is not yet available in official state reports; this information comes from secondary media coverage.

3. **Terminology shift:** Connecticut now preferentially uses "English Learner/Multilingual Learner" (EL/ML) over "English Language Learner" (ELL), though both remain in use in older documentation.

4. **Reclassification/monitoring status unclear:** The available administrative guidelines do not explicitly state whether Connecticut's published EL count includes students in a post-reclassification monitoring period. NCES explicitly excludes such students. If Connecticut includes them, the figures would not be directly comparable to the NCES series.

5. **Total enrollment figures:** 2024-25 total K-12 enrollment was 508,402. 2023-24 enrollment was 512,652. The 2024-25 enrollment represents a decrease of 0.83 percent year-over-year.
