## Quoted findings

- **EL population**: "Delaware's schools have approximately 14,000
  ELs, which mirrors the nation's average EL population of 10
  percent" (line 129).
- **Annual ELP assessment**: "Annual WIDA ACCESS for ELLs 2.0
  assessment" (line 644) — Delaware is a WIDA Consortium state and
  uses ACCESS for ELLs 2.0 (and Alternate ACCESS for students with
  significant cognitive disabilities).
- **Service models** (lines 1290-1340; 3370-3420): explicitly named:
  "Dual Language Immersion: Push-In / Pull-Out" (EL-certified teacher
  required), "ESL" (EL teacher provides language support), and
  "Sheltered English Instruction" — described as
  > "Content certified teachers who have received training on
  > strategies to make subject-area content rigorous, culturally
  > relevant, and comprehensible for ELs."
- **SIOP** is named as the Sheltered Instruction model used (lines
  1688-1700).

## SEI mandate analysis

The guidebook treats Sheltered English Instruction as **one service
model among several** offered by content teachers who have received
SIOP-style training, not as a state mandate that every Delaware
teacher complete SEI/sheltered instruction coursework or training as
a condition of certification or assignment.

The closest "all teachers" language reads:

> "All teachers (including those offering specialized classes such as
> Gym, Technology, electives, etc.) should review and understand the
> plan and implement it within the school setting on a daily basis."
> (line 1479; refers to the EL Plan, not to a sheltered-instruction
> credential mandate.)

Delaware does **not** have a statewide SEI endorsement requirement on
the AZ/CA/MA model. There is no codified "all teachers must complete
N hours of SEI training" rule comparable to MA's RETELL, AZ's
SEI endorsement, or CA's CLAD/BCLAD. SEI is offered as one of
several service models within an LEA's EL plan.
