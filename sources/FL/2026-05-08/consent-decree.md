# LULAC v. State Board of Education Consent Decree (1990)

- URL: <https://www.fldoe.org/academics/eng-language-learners/consent-decree.stml>
- Retrieved: 2026-05-08
- Effective: 1990-08-14

## Overview

The Consent Decree is the foundational settlement establishing
Florida's ELL framework. Six sections cover identification &
assessment, equal access to programming, equal access to categorical
programs, **personnel requirements**, monitoring, and outcome measures.

## Personnel / teacher categories

The Decree mandates ESOL training for *all* certificated personnel who
serve ELLs, with required training scaled by role. The four categories
(per FLDOE-aligned district guidance):

- **Category I** — Teachers of Language Arts/English/Reading,
  Elementary, and ESE who provide reading or English to ELLs: full
  ESOL endorsement (15 SH or 300 inservice points across five areas)
  *or* pass the FTCE 047 K-12 ESOL exam plus 2 ESOL courses.
- **Category II** — Teachers of math, science, social studies,
  computer literacy: 1 approved 3-SH ESOL course or 60 inservice
  points (the "ESOL-Infused" requirement).
- **Category III** — Teachers of fine arts/electives, speech-language
  pathologists, OT/PT: 1 course or 18 inservice points.
- **Category IV** — School-based administrators and school counselors:
  1 course or 60 inservice points (per district practice).

## Implications for our schema

- The Decree creates a *de facto* requirement that every Florida
  classroom teacher of ELLs in covered subjects receive ESOL training,
  but it does NOT mandate sheltered-English training for *all*
  teachers regardless of role/student population. Cat-I+II coverage is
  conditional on actually teaching ELLs.
- Therefore `sei.mandatedForAllTeachers` remains `false` (FL is not in
  the AZ/CA/MA "all teachers, regardless of student" club).
- The Decree is the reason FL's ESOL endorsement architecture exists
  in its current form — preserve in `notes` per project rule.
