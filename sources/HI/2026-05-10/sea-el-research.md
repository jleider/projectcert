## SEA methodology

The Hawaii Department of Education defines its EL population through a two-step process:
1. Students entering public school whose families indicate home language use beyond English receive an English proficiency assessment.
2. Classification includes three statuses:
   - **Active EL**: Students not yet proficient, receiving support services (approximately 7% statewide; up to 22% in some geographic areas)
   - **Monitored EL**: Former active students meeting proficiency standards, tracked for two years
   - **Former EL**: Students completing the two-year monitoring period

The broader EL population (including all three statuses) represents approximately 17–18% of Hawaii's public school students.

The EL program is grounded in the 1970 Office for Civil Rights Memorandum establishing affirmative obligation for schools to address language barriers. Hawaii's EL students represent approximately 70 different languages annually, with Ilokano and Chuukese being the most prevalent statewide.

Snapshot month is not explicitly specified in available publications; NCES data uses October (fall) snapshots.

## Notes / caveats

1. **Distinguishing classification from active enrollment**: Hawaii's EL sources frequently cite "17% or 18% are or have been ELs" (a cumulative classification metric including monitored and former-EL students) alongside "7% receive active EL services" (current enrollment in EL programs). This distinction mirrors the NCES definition but Hawaii does not appear to publish the classification-only figure in downloadable state-level tables.

2. **No recent SEA publication**: The most recent year-by-year EL data available from the Hawaii SEA is embedded in aggregate descriptions ("17–18%" and "7%") rather than published as discrete enrollment tables for 2022–2025. This differs from states that issue annual fact sheets or dashboard updates with school-year-specific percentages.

3. **Data intermediary (Hawaii DXP)**: Much of Hawaii's reported EL data flows through the Hawaii Data eXchange Partnership (hawaiidxp.org), which partners with HIDOE but is not the direct publisher. The "Hawaii EL Data Story" is the primary public resource but does not expose year-by-year tabular data via web (interactive only).

4. **Contact pathway**: HIDOE directs research inquiries to:
   - Data Governance and Analysis Branch: 808-784-6061 (phone)
   - General HIDOE: 808-784-6200

5. **Total enrollment for denominator**: Hawaii's public and charter school enrollment is available from press releases:
   - 2023-24: 167,649 students
   - 2024-25: 165,340 students (1.4% decline)
   However, no matching year-to-year EL count was published alongside these figures.
