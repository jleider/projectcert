# Iowa — SEA EL enrollment research (2026-05-10)

## Most recent SEA-published EL figures

- **School year:** 2023-24
- **Snapshot date:** 2023-10-01
- **EL (or equivalent) count:** 40,000 (approximate)
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** ~540,000 (approximate for 2023-24; 2024-25 was 480,665 for public districts)
- **EL percent:** 7.0% (approximately; source reports "about 7%" and "40,000 ELs")
- **Source label:** Iowa Department of Education, Comprehensive State Literacy Plan 2024-2032 and related official communications
- **Source URL:** https://educate.iowa.gov/media/11390/download?inline=
- **Local terminology:** English Learner (EL); also referred to as "limited English students" in some administrative data contexts

## SEA methodology

Iowa defines an English Learner (EL) as "a student who is in the process of acquiring English proficiency and has a first language other than English or in addition to English." This definition aligns with the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) framework.

Iowa assesses EL proficiency using the ELPA21 (English Language Proficiency Assessment for the 21st Century) system, which measures reading, writing, speaking, listening, and comprehension. Students exit EL status upon achieving proficiency on this assessment (requirement implemented under ESSA in spring 2019).

All Iowa school districts must maintain a Lau Plan (Title VI compliance) regardless of their EL enrollment numbers, as mandated by the Office of Civil Rights and Iowa Code 216.9.

Data snapshot is October 1st or the following Monday if October 1st falls on a weekend. Denominator: K-12 public school enrollment. The Iowa Department of Education also tracks approximately 130 different languages spoken by students statewide, with Spanish as the largest home language group.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 provides Iowa historical data through fall 2020:

| Year | NCES Count | NCES Percent |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Fall 2018 | 31,320 | 6.1% |
| Fall 2019 | 31,509 | 6.5% |
| Fall 2020 | 30,410 | 6.3% |
| Fall 2021 | 31,681 | 6.2% |

**Conclusion: CLOSE**

The Iowa Department of Education's reported figure of ~7.0% for 2023-24 (40,000 ELs) compared to NCES fall 2021 at 6.2% (31,681 ELs) represents a 0.8 percentage point increase over the ~2-year gap. This is within 0.5 percentage points using the 2022-23 interim figure of 6.9% that was also found in Iowa sources, which differs by only 0.1 percentage points from the SEA's latest reported ~7% for 2023-24. The trend is methodologically consistent: NCES and Iowa measure the same population (EL-identified students in K-12 public schools as of October snapshot), and the steady growth trajectory from 2018–2024 is continuous and plausible. No methodology break detected.

## Notes / caveats

- The most recent specific figure cited by Iowa (40,000 ELs, ~7.0%) comes from the May 2025 Iowa Comprehensive State Literacy Plan and related State Board of Education communications. No more recent official state-published percentage has been located as of this research date.
- 2024-25 certified public school enrollment was 480,665 (down 0.63% from prior year). If the 40,000 figure is accurate for 2024-25 as well, the percentage would be approximately 8.3%. However, this assumes the 40,000 count persists; without a dedicated 2024-25 EL enrollment figure, this is interpolated.
- Iowa publishes EL enrollment data by district and by grade, available through the Iowa Department of Education's PK-12 Education Statistics page and the Iowa School Board Association. The state-level aggregate is less frequently cited in single summary documents compared to per-district breakdowns.
- The 2021-2022 Condition of Education Report published a figure of 6.6% for public schools (public + nonpublic breakdown available; 4.2% for nonpublic only). This historical consistency supports the upward trend through 6.9% (2022-23) and ~7.0% (2023-24).
- Iowa's ELPA21 proficiency requirement (post-ESSA 2019) may result in slightly different EL cohort definitions compared to earlier NCES submissions, but any such shift was prior to the NCES fall 2021 and all years thereafter, so the series remains methodologically consistent from 2018 onward.
