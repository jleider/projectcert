## SEA methodology

Indiana identifies and reports English Learners through its Department of Education. The state publishes annual EL enrollment counts through the English Learning and Migrant Education office and makes per-corporation and per-school disaggregated data available through its Data Center & Reports portal (updated for SY 2025-2026). 

The precise definition of EL identification (e.g., whether it includes reclassified/former-EL students in a monitoring period) is documented in the IDOE EL Guidebook and WIDA's "Indiana English Learner Identification and Placement Guidance" but those documents were not fully accessible during this research. The state appears to use WIDA assessments for EL identification, consistent with multi-state practice.

Snapshot date: The 103,024 figure for 2024-2025 was reported in late December 2024 by media citing Indiana Department of Education records. The exact snapshot date (October, November, or another month) is not explicitly stated in the sources accessed.

## Notes / caveats

1. **Source reliability:** The 103,024 and 83,855 figures come from Indiana Department of Education records as reported by The Republic News (December 21, 2024). The news outlet explicitly cited "records with the Indiana Department of Education," but I could not access the primary IDOE document directly (the "2024-2025 Public English Learner Counts" PDF from in.gov was not readable during this research). The figures are therefore credible but not directly verified against the original IDOE source.

2. **Calculated percentage:** The 103,024 figure appears consistent with the reported growth arc. If Indiana's total K-12 public enrollment remains stable around 1,030,000–1,035,000 (Fall 2023 NCES Digest reports 1,032,700), then 103,024 ÷ ~1,032,000 ≈ 10.0%. This is an extrapolation, not an official Indiana SEA figure.

3. **Snapshot date ambiguity:** Neither the 103,024 nor the 83,855 figure explicitly states a snapshot date. NCES uses October fall-enrollment snapshots; Indiana may use a different month or cumulative count. This methodological difference is not documented in accessible sources.

4. **Denominator scope:** NCES uses K-12 public-school denominator per CCD (Common Core Data). Indiana's published counts appear to be K-12 public schools only, but the exact scope (e.g., whether they include charter schools, virtual schools, or LEAs operated by the state) is not confirmed in the sources accessed.

5. **Definition of EL:** Indiana's EL definition (whether it includes reclassified/former-EL students in a monitoring period, how they handle dual-language immersion students, etc.) could not be directly verified. The WIDA and IDOE guidebooks reference this but were not readable. This is a known gap.

6. **Growth rate claim:** The reported "22% increase over the past three school years" (83,855 → 103,024) checks mathematically: (103,024 − 83,855) / 83,855 = 0.229 ≈ 22.9%, consistent with the 22% figure cited in reporting.

7. **Why NO OVERLAP:** The Indiana DOE does maintain historical EL enrollment tables, but accessing the state-level aggregated percentages for 2018-2021 would require either (a) downloading and parsing the Excel files directly from the data center, or (b) finding a report that already aggregated those years. Neither was available through web search and WebFetch during this research. The NCES data is the only reliable baseline for those years.
