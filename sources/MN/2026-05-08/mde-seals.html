Minnesota Bilingual Seals Program

2024 Legislative Impacts on the Minnesota Bilingual Seals. 

Minnesota Bilingual and Multilingual Seals were legislated in the Learning English for Academic Proficiency and Success (LEAPS) Act in 2014. Minnesota public school districts, charter schools, and nonpublic schools may award Minnesota bilingual and multilingual seals to high school students upon graduation who have demonstrated the required levels of language proficiency through assessment in languages other than English, including American Sign Language (ASL) and Indigenous American Indian languages. The required proficiency levels are overall ACTFL Advanced Low for a Platinum Seal; overall ACTFL Intermediate High for a Gold Seal demonstrated through assessments in all of the four modalities—listening, reading, speaking and writing.  *Note: Minnesota State Colleges and Universities award college credit only when the test was taken in grades 10, 11, or 12. Therefore, it is not recommended to take an assessment in grade 9 if the goal is to obtain college credit.  

 

Minnesota World Language Proficiency Certificate, legislated in 2009, is an award given to students in any grade level who demonstrate an overall ACTFL’s Intermediate Low and Mid level demonstrated through assessments in all of the four modalities—listening, reading, speaking and writing in languages other than in English. *Note: Minnesota State Colleges and Universities award college credit when the test was taken in grades 10, 11, or 12.


Minnesota State Colleges and Universities, a statewide system of 30 colleges and seven universities with 54 campuses across the state, compiled a report that provides findings and recommendations for the granting of college credit for the bilingual and multilingual seals and world language proficiency certificates.

Award levels and the number of college credit

The actual number of college credits may vary depending on an institution. Each institution has a different number of credits per a semester course.

Award Criteria
Overall proficiency of listening,
reading, speaking and writing		 Number of college credits earned
Bilingual Platinum Seal ACTFL Advanced Low 4 semesters
Bilingual Gold Seal ACTFL Intermediate High 3 semesters
World Language Proficiency Certificate ACTFL Intermediate Low and Mid 2 semesters

In order to qualify for a bilingual seal, a student must also demonstrate proficiency in English by having met the required English Language Arts credit for high school graduation. The World Language Proficiency Certificate does not have English Language Arts credit requirements.

How to access college credits

  • Students must be enrolled in a Minnesota State Colleges and Universities* institution. * Note the University of Minnesota is an independent system that is not part of the Minnesota State Colleges and Universities system. 
  • Students must make a request for college credit. Students should work with the admission office or advisor. In some cases, scholarship may be impacted by receiving extra credit. Therefore, students should consult with appropriate personnel or offices at the institution.
  • Students have taken a test when in grades 10, 11, or 12.
  • Students may be required to submit an official high school transcript.
  • There is a three-year window to make a request after a high school graduation.

Approved Assessment for Minnesota Bilingual Seals

Students must demonstrate proficiency through assessments. Bilingual Seals and World Language Proficiency Certificate are tied to college credit; therefore, the integrity of the assessment administration is important. These assessments should be treated as such where the students could demonstrate their language proficiency on their own without any assistance. There are a number of assessments that are qualified for earning Minnesota Bilingual, Multilingual Seals, and World Language Proficiency Certificate.

These are widely used assessments by Minnesota schools.
  • MN Bilingual Seals Assessment through Extempore (Release date - first week of January)
  • Amharic - reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMP WS.)
  • Dakota - reading, listening, speaking and writing
  • Hmong - reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMP WS.)
  • Karen - reading, listening, speaking, and writing
  • Ojibwe - reading, listening, speaking, and writing
  • Oromo - reading, listening, speaking, and writing
  • Somali – reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMPWS.)
  • Vietnamese - reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMPWS.)
  • Dari - reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use ACTFL SOBL bundle or AVANT SuperLanguageTest.) 
  • Pashto - reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMP WS.)
  • Swahili – reading and listening (For speaking and writing use AVANT STAMP WS).
  • Ukrainian - reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMP WS.)
  • Tamil – reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMP WS.)
  • Telugu - reading and listening (For speaking and writing, use AVANT STAMP WS.)
  • Slovak – reading (For other modalities, contact mde.worldlang@state.mn.us 

For languages such as Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, please visit ACTFL Assessment of Performance toward Proficiency in Languages or AVANT STAMP4S. 

The Global Seal of Biliteracy 
The Global Seal of Biliteracy is an independent organization that certifies individuals’ language skills. The criteria and requirements are different from Minnesota Bilingual Seals program. There are many languages that are available through the Global Seal of Biliteracy that are not available through the Minnesota Bilingual Seals. For some languages that are not listed above, the Global Seal might be an option.  MDE does not facilitate nor assist with the application to the Global Seal. Please work with them directly.  The Minnesota Statutes 120B.022 does not specify whether the Minnesota State Colleges and Universities should award credits. Families and students are always encouraged to inquire at the enrolled institutions. 
 

Responsibilities of Districts and Schools

Minnesota Bilingual and Multilingual Seals and World Language Proficiency Certificates are voluntary programs. Schools and districts are strongly encouraged to develop infrastructure to support the implementation of Bilingual and Multilingual Seals and World Language Proficiency Certificate. Implementation of the Bilingual Seals program demonstrates the commitment for equity by elevating the status of linguistic assets other than English that many of our Minnesota students and families possess. Simultaneously, it encourages monolingual English speakers to learn languages other than their own. Biliteracy and bilingualism are for everyone. Strategic implementation of the Bilingual Seals program will bring all learners together for the common goal of biliteracy and bilingualism where learners could develop the valuable linguistic skills, characters and dispositions that are necessary to live and work with people who are different from their own.   

School districts and charter schools have the following responsibilities in the implementation of the Bilingual Seals Program:
  1. Promotion and advertisement to recruit and identify candidates.
  2. Assessment administration
  3. Awardee identification
  4. Seals and proficiency certificate request to the Minnesota Department of Education (MDE)
  5. Record keeping
  6. Transcript notation *Note that affixing a seal on a high school diploma is not required by law.  
  7. Informing families and students
To learn more about the specificities of each step in the process, please email mde.worldlang@state.mn.us to obtain more information or register for the monthly webinar. Registration information is below.

Requesting the templates for the Bilingual Seals, Multilingual Seals, and World Language Proficiency Certificate

Bilingual Seals, Multilingual Seals and World Language Proficiency Certificates are the state’s official awards. The certificate templates are updated with a new date each year. Please make a request to obtain the templates each spring by submitting a request form.

In the following situations, you can obtain the certificate templates without submitting the above form by filling out this certificate template request form after April 1 of each year. 
  • You are preparing for a senior event to recognize the awardees and working through tight turnaround time to receive the results or waiting for the results for some but you have the results for others. 
  • You are going to send a request form as a report after you find out about AP Language and Culture exams and IBDP exams in late summer or fall. 
Reporting to the MDE
MDE collects data on the number of awardees by the award levels, languages, English Learner status, and used assessments. Currently, this information is collected through the request form you submit.  The due date for this form is October 31 in each year for the previous school year.
