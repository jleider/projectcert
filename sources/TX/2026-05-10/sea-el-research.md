# Texas — SEA EL/EB enrollment research (2026-05-10)

## Most recent SEA-published EB/EL figures

- **School year:** 2024-25
- **Snapshot date:** 2024-10-25 (last Friday of October 2024)
- **EB/EL count:** 1,347,254
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** 5,544,255
- **EB/EL percent:** 24.3%
- **Source label:** Texas Education Agency, *Enrollment in Texas Public Schools 2024-25*
- **Source URL:** https://tea.texas.gov/reports-and-data/school-performance/accountability-research/enroll-2024-25.pdf

## SEA methodology

Texas Education Agency (TEA) defines students as **Emergent Bilingual (EB)** per Texas Education Code §29.051–29.064 and the Language Proficiency Assessment Committee (LPAC) framework. Students are identified as emergent bilingual if they do not speak English as their primary language, as determined by home-language surveys and formal English proficiency assessments. This is a classification based on student identification, not program enrollment.

The denominator is **K-12 public-school enrollment only** (grades K through 12), excluding pre-K and state-administered districts.

**Snapshot date:** PEIMS data are collected as of the **last Friday in October** each school year. For the 2024-25 school year, this was **2024-10-25**. This is the standard TEA enrollment reporting date.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 reported the following for Texas (EL percentage):
- Fall 2018: 17.8%
- Fall 2019: 18.6%
- Fall 2020: 19.3%
- Fall 2021: 20.2%

TEA's own historical series (from the 2024-25 report):
- 2014-15: 18.1%
- 2023-24: 24.3%
- 2024-25: 24.3%

**Divergence note:** The TEA percentage (24.3%) is notably higher than NCES's fall 2021 figure (20.2%), a gap of ~4.1 percentage points. This likely reflects both genuine growth in the EB population and methodological differences in identification practices between the two agencies. NCES may use a narrower definition (active EL vs. all identified EB), or states may have refined their LPAC processes post-2021. The trajectory is consistent: both sources show steady growth from 2018–2024.

## Notes / caveats

1. **Statutory terminology shift:** Texas formally replaced "English Learner (EL)" with "Emergent Bilingual (EB)" via Senate Bill 2066 (effective 2021). Both terms refer to the same population under LAPAC identification. TEA now consistently uses "Emergent Bilingual" in all publications.

2. **Classification vs. program enrollment:** The EB count reported here (1,347,254) represents students *identified as emergent bilingual* according to LPAC criteria. This is distinct from bilingual or ESL *program enrollment*. Some EB students may not participate in bilingual/ESL programs (and may instead be in mainstream classes), while some native English speakers may participate in dual-language programs. The 2024-25 report notes that 77.2% of EB students participated in state-approved bilingual or ESL models, and 18.8% participated in alternative instructional models.

3. **Denominator includes charter schools:** Total enrollment includes students in regular public school districts, state-authorized charter schools (7.9% of the total), and district-authorized charter school campuses (1.1% of the total).

4. **Recent growth:** The EB percentage has grown from 18.1% (2014-15) to 24.3% (2024-25)—an increase of 6.2 percentage points over a decade, reflecting broader demographic shifts in Texas's student population.
