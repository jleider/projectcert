## SEA methodology

Alaska defines English learners through statewide entrance procedures per Title III-A guidance. An English learner (EL) is identified as someone aged 3-21 who was not born in the United States or whose native language differs from English, and whose "difficulties in speaking, reading, writing, or understanding the English language" may impede academic success.

Alaska adopted the WIDA English Language Development Standards Framework in 2021 for instruction and assessment. All school districts must establish identification procedures, including home language surveys. Districts with eight or more English learners at a single site must file an approved EL Plan of Service.

The denominator is K-12 public school enrollment only. The snapshot date is October 1 of each fall (October 2021 for the data cited above).

## Comparison to NCES

The most recent Alaska data available is from Fall 2021 (10.8%), which matches exactly the NCES baseline value of 10.8% provided for that year. No newer data beyond Fall 2021 has been published by either the Alaska Department of Education & Early Development or NCES as of May 2026.

For the overlapping NCES years (Fall 2018-2021) provided in the baseline:

- **Fall 2018:** No Alaska-specific SEA comparison data found
- **Fall 2019:** No Alaska-specific SEA comparison data found
- **Fall 2020:** NCES reported 13,894 ELs (11.0%); Fall 2021 showed 14,017 ELs (10.8%)
- **Fall 2021:** NCES = 10.8%; Alaska DEED reporting aligns with NCES

**Conclusion: NO OVERLAP** — The Alaska Department of Education & Early Development does not appear to publish multi-year EL enrollment tables in a publicly accessible format on their website or data portal as of May 2026. The only verified Alaska figure is Fall 2021 (10.8%), sourced via NCES Digest, which matches the NCES value exactly.

## Notes / caveats

- The Alaska Department of Education & Early Development maintains an EL data portal and reporting system (Compass), but public-facing enrollment percentage figures for 2022, 2023, or 2024 are not publicly available online.
- The policy research literature notes that Alaska classifies students as EL at unusually high rates relative to students' actual English proficiency (12.1% receive EL services vs. 2.5% speaking English less than "very well"), suggesting Alaska's definition is broader than typical.
- No newer data was found despite searches of the Alaska DEED Data Center, NCES Digest dashboards, and state accountability reporting systems (Compass, Report Card to the Public).
