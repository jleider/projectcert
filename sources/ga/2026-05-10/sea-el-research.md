# Georgia — SEA EL enrollment research (2026-05-10)

## Most recent SEA-published EL figures

**No newer data found.** Georgia Department of Education does not appear to publish state-level EL enrollment percentages in a standard public report or dashboard for years after 2019. The most recent SEA-published figure located is from FY 2019.

- **School year:** FY 2019
- **Snapshot date:** 2019-10-01 (October FTE count)
- **EL (or equivalent) count:** 108,752
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** ~1,726,000 (calculated from 6.3%)
- **EL percent:** 6.3%
- **Source label:** Georgia Budget and Policy Institute (GBPI) fact sheet
- **Source URL:** https://gbpi.org/fact-sheet-english-for-speakers-of-other-languages/
- **Local terminology:** ESOL (English to Speakers of Other Languages)

## SEA methodology

Georgia's State Education Agency (GaDOE) defines English Learners as students whose native language is not English and who have difficulty speaking, reading, writing, or understanding the English language (Title 34 Code of State Rules and Regulations, Chapter 160-3-1).

**Denominator:** K-12 public school enrollment. Georgia counts students enrolled in the state-funded ESOL program, defined as enrollment in at least one 50-60 minute daily segment.

**Snapshot date:** October FTE (Full-Time Equivalent) count, coinciding with NCES reporting period.

**Definition variant:** Georgia's "percent of students with limited English proficiency" is calculated as the count of students identified as EL divided by the count of students identified at that school, system, or state anytime during the academic year according to Student Records. This differs from direct ESOL program enrollment and may capture broader identification than active program participation.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 historical values for Georgia:

- Fall 2018: 6.8%
- Fall 2019: 7.3%
- Fall 2020: 7.5%
- Fall 2021: 7.7% (projected; not directly published by NCES for this comparison)

**Comparison result: DIVERGES**

- Fall 2019: NCES reports 7.3%; GBPI/SEA reports 6.3% for FY 2019 (difference = 1.0 percentage point). This divergence exceeds the 0.5 pp threshold.

**Interpretation:**

The 1.0 pp gap between NCES (7.3%) and the GBPI-sourced SEA figure (6.3%) for the same school year (fall 2019) suggests one or both of the following:

1. **Methodological difference:** NCES (EDFacts Title III submissions to CCD) may count a broader EL-equivalent population (e.g., including reclassified/monitoring-period students) than Georgia's ESOL program enrollment figure. The GBPI sources emphasize "ESOL program enrollment" (students in ≥50-60 min/day service), which is narrower than cumulative EL *identification* across the state.

2. **Definition inconsistency:** No current SEA source explicitly states whether the GBPI-published 6.3% includes or excludes monitoring-period reclassified students. NCES does not exclude them; Georgia's sources are silent on this.

**Search scope (no overlap found):** 

Georgia's Department of Education does not publish a multi-year EL enrollment series on its public website (gosa.georgia.gov, gadoe.org, georgiainsights.gadoe.org, or the K-12 Report Card dashboard). The 2023-2024 school year data, when made available, would normally appear in:
- Governor's Office of Student Achievement (GOSA) K-12 Report Card (Student & School Demographics section)
- Georgia Insights GaDOE dashboard
- Downloadable data portal at gosa.georgia.gov

None of these sources returned public-facing FY 2021, FY 2022, or FY 2023 enrollment percentages during this research. **As of May 2026, it appears no newer state-level data has been published beyond FY 2019.**

## Notes / caveats

1. **GBPI vs. direct GaDOE source:** The 6.3% figure is sourced from the Georgia Budget and Policy Institute's fact sheet, which cites GaDOE data but is a secondary source. A direct GaDOE publication of the same figure was not located.

2. **Program enrollment vs. classification:** The 6.3% appears to reflect ESOL *program enrollment* (students in daily language service), not cumulative EL *identification* across the state. NCES reports the latter (or a variant including Title III eligible students). This accounts for much or all of the 1.0 pp gap.

3. **Monitoring-period students:** Neither the GBPI source nor any GaDOE source encountered during this research explicitly states whether reclassified/monitoring-period EL students are counted. NCES includes them; Georgia's reported figures may not.

4. **No 2024 data access:** Despite multiple attempts to access GOSA dashboards, Georgia Insights, and downloadable datasets, no 2023-24 or 2024-25 school year percentage data was publicly retrievable. The K-12 Report Card system appears to require interactive access not available via web search or direct URL fetches (SSL certificate issues encountered on gaawards.gosa.ga.gov).

5. **Extrapolation risk:** The historical trend (NCES: 6.8% → 7.3% → 7.5% → 7.7% from fall 2018–2021) suggests Georgia's EL enrollment has been growing, but no SEA data to confirm continuation of that trend into 2022–2024 exists in public sources.
