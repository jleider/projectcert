## SEA methodology

Massachusetts defines an English Learner per M.G.L. c. 71A as a student who does not speak English, or whose native language is not English, and who is not able to do ordinary classroom work in English. Identification occurs through a home language survey; students with a home language other than English are screened using WIDA (World-Class Instructional Design and Assessment) assessments in reading, writing, speaking, and listening. The denominator is K-12 public school enrollment only. Massachusetts reports this data through its School and District Profiles system (https://profiles.doe.mass.edu/), which aggregates enrollment by selected populations annually. The snapshot date follows NCES convention (October for K-12 fall enrollment, though Massachusetts SEA does not explicitly publish the exact snapshot date—this follows federal EDFacts/CCD submission protocols).

**Conclusion: NO OVERLAP** — The Massachusetts SEA does not publish historical EL enrollment percentages for the 2018–2021 period in its current public data systems. The earliest year found in the SEA's publicly accessible Selected Populations reports is 2022-23 (12.1%). NCES Table 204.20 ends at fall 2021 (10.5%). There is no overlap year to compare methodologies.

The jump from NCES fall 2021 (10.5%) to SEA fall 2023 (13.1%) represents an increase of 2.6 percentage points over two years. This magnitude of change is plausible given (1) demographic shifts in the state, (2) potential differences in identification/counting methodology between NCES (Title III submissions) and SEA internal records, and (3) possible reclassification practices. **Without overlapping years and explicit documentation of methodology differences, the continuity between the NCES series and the SEA series cannot be confirmed.**

## Notes / caveats

1. **Snapshot date precision:** Massachusetts DESE publishes enrollment data for "2023-24" without specifying an exact month. The inferred October 1, 2023 snapshot follows NCES CCD convention, but the SEA sources do not confirm this.

2. **Comparison gap:** The 2.6 percentage-point increase from NCES fall 2021 (10.5%) to MA SEA fall 2023 (13.1%) is large. Possible causes:
   - Demographic in-migration of EL students (plausible given labor market/immigration patterns in Massachusetts).
   - Methodological differences between NCES Title III (program participation) and SEA identification (EL status regardless of program enrollment). The SEA definition emphasizes classification, not program enrollment.
   - Reclassification practices or changes in the home-language survey administration over the 2021–2023 interval.

3. **No current/former EL distinction noted:** The MA SEA "English Learner" category in their Selected Populations reports does not explicitly state whether it includes reclassified/former-EL students in monitoring status. NCES only counts currently classified ELs. This is a potential source of methodological divergence.

4. **Denominator scope:** Both NCES and MA SEA report K-12 public school enrollment only. However, confirmation that both exclude pre-K is not explicit in all SEA publications reviewed.

5. **Most recent year:** The 2023-24 figures (13.1%) are the most recent published by the Massachusetts SEA at the time of this research (2026-05-10). No 2024-25 data found in public SEA sources. The 2025-26 Selected Populations report shows 13.4%, but the year-to-year change is modest (0.3 percentage points), consistent with plausible trend continuation.
