## SEA methodology

Maryland identifies English learners (referred to as Multilingual Learners) as students who may have been born outside the United States, communicate in a language other than English, or whose family uses a primary language other than English in the home, and whose English language proficiency falls within the range established by the State for an English language development program. 

Local education agencies conduct a Home Language Survey (HLS) upon enrollment to identify potential multilingual learners. Maryland uses the WIDA Screener for initial identification and WIDA ACCESS for MLLs to assess English language proficiency annually. Exit criteria require an overall composite proficiency level of 4.5 or above for K-12 students.

The snapshot date is September 30, 2023 (standard annual reporting). The denominator is total K-12 public school enrollment, consistent with NCES CCD methodology.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 data for Maryland (fall snapshots):
- Fall 2018: 84,120 ELs (9.4%)
- Fall 2019: 93,249 ELs (10.3%)
- Fall 2020: 88,834 ELs (10.1%)
- Fall 2021: 98,575 ELs (11.2%)

SEA-published data for overlapping years:
- Fall 2020: 88,834 (NCELA data)
- Fall 2021: 98,566 (NCELA data)

**Comparison conclusion: CLOSE** 

Fall 2020 values match exactly (88,834). Fall 2021 values differ by 9 students (98,575 NCES vs. 98,566 NCELA/SEA), a difference of 0.009% — well within the 0.2 pp threshold. The 2023-24 SEA figure (112,081 ELs; 12.59%) represents a continuation of the upward trend observed in the NCES series and is methodologically continuous with prior years.

## Notes / caveats

- The 2023-24 figure (112,081) comes from the Maryland Department of Education's Office of Multilingual Education EL enrollment report. The official PDF document is access-restricted but the count was verified through multiple search results and references.
- Total enrollment for 2023-24 (890,137) derived from secondary sources documenting September 30, 2023 snapshot enrollment; the SEA's official enrollment-by-race/ethnicity document was access-restricted.
- Maryland terminology has evolved: the SEA now prefers "Multilingual Learner (ML)" but continues to report EL counts and uses "English Learner" terminology in public documents.
- The NCELA website shows Fall 2020-21 and 2021-22 counts (88,834 and 98,566 respectively), confirming continuity with NCES historical series.
- No evidence of inclusion of reclassified/monitoring students in the SEA count beyond what NCES includes; methodology appears aligned.
