# Missouri — SEA EL enrollment research (2026-05-10)

- **School year:** 2024-25
- **Snapshot date:** Not explicitly published; estimated fall 2024
- **EL (or equivalent) count:** Approximately 40,000
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** 896,167 (2024-25, fully-accredited public schools; higher total includes charter and non-accredited)
- **EL percent:** 4.5%–4.6% (estimated range based on 40,000 ÷ 896,167)
- **Source label:** Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education; Missourinet article (May 2024)
- **Source URL:** https://www.missourinet.com/2024/05/28/many-missouri-schools-lack-teachers-to-educate-english-language-learners/
- **Local terminology:** EL (English Learner); also used: LEP (Limited English Proficient) in federal Title III context; ELD (English Language Development) for instructional programs

## SEA methodology

**Definition:** Missouri's EL definition, per ESEA Section 3201 and state Title III regulations, encompasses individuals ages 3–21 enrolled in school whose English language difficulties may prevent them from meeting state achievement standards, succeeding in English-language classrooms, or participating fully in society. More specifically, students are identified as EL if they were not born in the United States or whose native language is not English, who are Native American or Alaska Native, or a native resident of U.S. outlying areas, and who come from an environment where a language other than English has had a significant impact on their English language proficiency.

**Identification process:** Districts screen students whose Language Use Survey (LUS) indicates a language other than English is spoken or understood. Screening uses the WIDA Screener or Missouri Newcomer Screener Kit. English language proficiency is assessed via ACCESS for ELLs assessment annually; proficiency is defined as a score of 4.7 or above.

**Denominator and snapshot date:** The 2024-25 Public Education Snapshot from DESE reports 896,167 students in fully-accredited public schools (>92% of total enrollment). Missouri's data system is MOSIS (Missouri Student Information System) and Core Data, which collect K-12 individual student-level data. The snapshot is based on October/fall enrollment data, consistent with NCES methodology. The most recent explicit state-level total mentioned in official DESE publications (the 2024-25 Public Education Snapshot) does not separately report EL classification counts.

## Comparison to NCES

**NCES Fall 2021 baseline (Digest Table 204.20):**
- Fall 2018: 34,269 ELs (3.8% of 901,816)
- Fall 2019: 34,219 ELs (3.8% of 899,842)
- Fall 2020: 32,512 ELs (3.7% of 878,661)
- Fall 2021: 34,159 ELs (3.8% of 896,817)

**SEA data found:**
- Approximately 40,000 ELs (2024-25, estimate ~4.5%–4.6% of ~896,000 total)

**Conclusion: NO OVERLAP for historical years; DIVERGES for implied 2024-25 estimate.**

Missouri's DESE does not publish a multi-year EL enrollment table for fall 2022, 2023, or 2024. The only recent state-level EL count I found was the ~40,000 figure cited in the May 2024 Missourinet article, which is measurably higher than the NCES 2021 figure (34,159). This 17.6% increase from 2021 to 2024-25 aligns with anecdotal reporting of growing EL populations in districts like Lee's Summit (ELL tripled 2020–2024) and the mention of "nearly 40,000 students whose primary language is not English" in 2024.

**Why no direct comparison:**
- Missouri DESE does not appear to publish state-level EL enrollment tables covering the 2022–2024 period with sufficient transparency to enable year-by-year NCES comparison.
- The MCDS (Missouri Comprehensive Data System) portal exists at `apps.dese.mo.gov/MCDS` but requires authentication.
- Public-facing resources (DESE School Data portal, enrollment reports, public dashboards) do not prominently surface state-level EL totals.
- Federal Title III funding allocation documents list per-district allocations but not aggregate EL counts.

**Methodological continuity assessment:**
The ~40,000 figure likely reflects the same identification methodology (WIDA-based screening, ACCESS annual assessment) that NCES records, suggesting continuity. However, without a transparent state-level time-series published by DESE, I cannot definitively rule out changes in scope (e.g., inclusion of formerly-EL students in monitoring status, which NCES excludes). The gap and apparent growth warrant explicit DESE clarification.

## Notes / caveats

1. **No explicit state total in public DESE reports.** The 40,000 figure comes from a Missourinet article citing DESE staff comments, not from an official DESE enrollment table or press release.

2. **Total enrollment denominator is ambiguous.** DESE's 2024-25 Public Education Snapshot reports 896,167 in "fully-accredited" public schools (>92% of total). Total public school enrollment including non-accredited schools is higher, but the exact figure was not found in accessible public sources.

3. **MOSIS data is not publicly aggregated at the state level.** Missouri's primary data system (MOSIS / Core Data) is the source of all school-reported EL classifications, but DESE does not appear to publish state-level summaries beyond the ~40,000 figure mentioned to media.

4. **Title III allocations do not substitute for enrollment counts.** The 2024-25 Title III English Learner Final Allocations document exists but was not fetched; DESE allocation formulae use prior-year EL counts but are not transparent in published form.

5. **Most recent NCES data is Fall 2021.** The Digest of Education Statistics 2023 (Table 204.20) ends at Fall 2021. No CCD data with state-level EL breakdowns for Fall 2022, 2023, or 2024 has been published by NCES yet, though Fall 2023 CCD universe files exist.

6. **Anecdotal district-level growth.** Lee's Summit R-7 reported ELL rising from <1% (2009) to 2.5% (2023-24), with enrollment nearly doubling 2020–2024. This growth pattern aligns with the state-level jump from 34,159 (2021) to ~40,000 (2024), but the statewide driver is not documented.
