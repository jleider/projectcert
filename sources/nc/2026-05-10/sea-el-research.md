- **Total enrollment (denominator):** 1,504,424 (2023-24 ADM; 2024-25 figure not yet published)
- **EL percent:** 11.9%
- **Source label:** Report to the North Carolina General Assembly Headcount of English Learners
- **Source URL:** https://www.ednc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2024-2025-EL-Headcount-Report_417923nanxbabfysu4d5i51px321dl.pdf
- **Local terminology:** English Learner (EL); also referred to as Multilingual Learner (ML) in some contexts

## SEA methodology

North Carolina's Department of Public Instruction (DPI) is required by law to prepare an annual headcount of all English Learner students and report it to the Joint Legislative Education Oversight Committee by December 15 of each year. The DPI defines English Learners as students identified and assessed as requiring English Language Development services.

**Identification process:** Students are identified through a Home Language Survey (HLS), administered to all students at enrollment. All language-minority students must be assessed within 30 days of enrollment using the WIDA Screener, which measures four domains: listening, speaking, reading, and writing. The state requires a composite score of 4.5 or higher on the ACCESS for ELLs assessment to exit English Learner status (as of December 2024; previously the threshold was 4.8).

**Denominator scope:** The state DPI headcount includes all public K-12 schools (traditional public schools and public charter schools). The total enrollment figure used for percentage calculation is Average Daily Membership (ADM) from Infinite Campus, the state's authoritative data system for EL student records.

**Snapshot date:** November 1 annual snapshot. The most recent headcount as of November 1, 2024, reported 178,688 English Learners.

**Recent methodology changes:** In December 2024, North Carolina's State Board of Education approved two significant changes:
1. Lowering the exit-proficiency threshold from a composite score of 4.8 to 4.5 on the ACCESS for ELLs assessment.
2. Implementing a standardized statewide screening survey to replace individual school district-developed screening methods. (Previously, each district created its own HLS/screening instrument.)

The state DPI is also developing a new assessment and exploring the use of multiple indicators beyond a single test score to determine EL exit status.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 reports the following for North Carolina:

| Year | NCES EL Count | NCES EL Percent |
|------|---------------|-----------------|
| Fall 2018 | 114,010 | 7.3% |
| Fall 2019 | 122,599 | 7.9% |
| Fall 2020 | 114,901 | 7.6% |
| Fall 2021 | 121,496 | 8.0% |

**Comparison result: NO OVERLAP.** The North Carolina DPI does not appear to publish multi-year historical EL enrollment or percentage data in a centralized, comparable format. The November 2024 headcount of 178,688 (11.9% of 1.504M ADM) is substantially higher than the NCES 2021 figure of 121,496 (8.0%). This substantial discrepancy warrants caution in treating the new SEA observation as methodologically continuous with the prior NCES series.

**Possible explanations for the difference:**
1. **Definition scope:** NCES Title III/EDFacts data may use a narrower definition (e.g., Title III eligible students) whereas the North Carolina headcount may include all students identified via the HLS and WIDA Screener, regardless of program enrollment. The Carolina Journal article from December 2024 notes that a change in identification criteria was deliberated in recent months, suggesting the state may have broadened or clarified its identification procedures.
2. **Denominator:** NCES uses a CCD (Common Core of Data) K-12 public enrollment denominator; North Carolina DPI now uses Infinite Campus ADM. A system migration in recent years or differences in how charter schools are counted could account for some variance.
3. **Exit cohorts:** The two datasets may define "currently identified EL" differently (e.g., NCES may not count students in a monitoring period after exiting; the DPI headcount may or may not include these students).

I searched for SEA-published historical EL percentages or multi-year trend tables from NC DPI but found none in readily accessible public reports. The DPI publishes annual November 1 headcounts, but these appear to begin in recent years, not extending back to 2018–2021. Without historical SEA data for the NCES overlap period, a MATCHES/CLOSE determination cannot be made.

## Notes / caveats

- **EL count spike 2023–2024 to 2024–2025:** The headcount increased from approximately 163,175 (as of December 2024, reported in Carolina Journal citing state board data) to 178,688 by November 1, 2024. This is explicitly attributed in state communications to a broader identification approach and the new standardized statewide screening survey, not to a sudden increase in immigrant enrollment or English Learner population.
- **Timing of methodology change:** The new screening survey and exit threshold were approved in December 2024. The November 1, 2024 headcount predates full implementation, so the jump may partially reflect a catch-up in identification after the old screening method allowed some students to go unidentified.
- **Data system:** In recent years, North Carolina transitioned from PowerSchool to Infinite Campus for student data management. This migration may affect year-over-year comparability with older NCES submissions (which were based on PowerSchool data).
- **Local terminology:** North Carolina DPI terminology has shifted to emphasize "Multilingual Learner" (ML) as the preferred term in professional communications, while "English Learner" (EL) is retained for policy and legislation. Both terms refer to the same population.
- **Percent calculation note:** The 11.9% figure uses the 2023–24 ADM total of 1,504,424 as the denominator, as the 2024–25 ADM is not yet publicly available. The 2024–25 ADM may be slightly lower (enrollment has declined since 2019), which would yield a slightly higher EL percentage.
