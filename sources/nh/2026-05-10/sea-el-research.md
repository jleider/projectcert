# New Hampshire — SEA EL enrollment research (2026-05-10)

## Most recent SEA-published EL figures

The New Hampshire Department of Education does not maintain a publicly accessible, state-level EL enrollment dashboard or report with specific year-over-year enrollment data paired with total enrollment denominators. The most recent figures found through SEA-related public reporting:

- **School year:** 2024-25
- **Snapshot date:** Unknown (September 2024 estimated from news articles)
- **EL (or equivalent) count:** ~5,000–5,500 (estimated from news reports; exact figure not verified)
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** ~160,000–162,660 (as of fall 2024)
- **EL percent:** ~3.1%–3.4% (calculated estimate; not SEA-published)
- **Source label:** News articles citing state data; no official SEA report found
- **Source URL:** None (SEA does not publish consolidated state-level EL enrollment reports)
- **Local terminology:** English Learner (EL), ESOL (English for Speakers of Other Languages), ESL (English as a Second Language)

## SEA methodology

The New Hampshire Department of Education defines English Learners through the WIDA (World-Class Instructional Design and Assessment) Consortium, which NH adopted in 2004. The state employs WIDA English language proficiency standards for identification and placement (see *Identification and Placement Guidance* document).

**Key definitions:**
- EL identification is based on home-language survey responses; students identified as EL include those currently receiving English language development services and those in monitoring status after reclassification.
- The denominator appears to be K–12 public school enrollment (including charter schools).
- Snapshot appears to be fall enrollment (September).

**Governance:** Title III funding flows through the Bureau of Instructional Support (Office of ESEA Title Programs). The state does not publish a single consolidated EL enrollment report.

## Notes / caveats

1. **No SEA-published EL enrollment series.** Unlike many states (e.g., New York, Texas), New Hampshire does not maintain a public, downloadable EL enrollment report or fact sheet with historical data. The state may track EL enrollment internally for Title III compliance and funding purposes, but this is not transparently published.

2. **Terminology variance.** The state uses EL, ESOL, and ESL interchangeably in public documents. The WIDA Consortium framework (adopted 2004) is the official identification standard.

3. **News reports vs. official data.** News articles (October 2023 / December 2024) reference "more than 5,000" EL students and roughly "5%" of public school students being designated English language learners, but these figures are not sourced to a specific SEA report and appear to be journalist estimates based on partial data (e.g., one source noted that Manchester alone serves 2,500 EL students, roughly 40% of the state total).

4. **Reclassified / monitoring students.** Unknown whether SEA or news reports include students in monitoring status post-reclassification. NCES definitions do not; SEA methodology unclear from available public documents.

5. **Fall 2021 is the latest verified baseline.** NCES Table 204.20 stops at fall 2021 (2.8%, n=4,822). NCES Common Core of Data (CCD) typically releases multi-year state summaries with a lag; fall 2023 or 2024 data may be available in EDFacts but was not accessible through public web search.
