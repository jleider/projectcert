# Ohio — SEA EL enrollment research (2026-05-10)

## Most recent SEA-published EL figures

- **School year:** 2023-24
- **Snapshot date:** Fall 2023 (October)
- **EL (or equivalent) count:** 77,152
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** 1,665,521
- **EL percent:** 4.6%
- **Source label:** Ohio's Education Landscape 2023-2024
- **Source URL:** https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Data/Frequently-Requested-Data/Facts-and-Figures/Ohios-Education-Landscape-2023-2024.pdf
- **Local terminology:** English Learner (EL); Ohio also uses "multilingual learner" in some contexts

## SEA methodology

Ohio's Department of Education and Workforce defines English learners per federal statute (20 U.S.C. 7801, referenced in Ohio Revised Code Section 3301.0731). The state identifies students who require academic English language support through a three-step standardized process:

1. **Language Usage Survey** — administered within 30 days of enrollment to all new students to identify those needing English support
2. **English Language Proficiency Assessment** — students whose home language is not English take the Ohio English Language Proficiency Screener (OELPS) to determine if targeted instruction is needed
3. **Parent notification** — families are notified within 30 calendar days of enrollment or within the first two weeks of program placement

**Denominator scope:** K-12 public school enrollment (excludes nonpublic/private schools). The 2023-24 figure uses the full public school ADM (Average Daily Membership) or enrollment count of 1,665,521 as the denominator. The snapshot date is October (fall), consistent with NCES reporting.

**Definition:** Ohio Revised Code references the federal ESEA definition. Ohio statutes recognize three categories of English learners for funding purposes, with different support multiples based on recency of enrollment in U.S. schools. The state collects EL data through EMIS (Education Management Information System), the statewide K-12 data system.

**Conclusion: NO OVERLAP.** Ohio Department of Education and Workforce does not publish historical EL enrollment breakdowns for fall 2018–2021 in the sources checked. NCES Table 204.20 stops at fall 2021 (3.8%, 63,879 students). The most recent SEA-published figure is fall 2023 (4.6%, 77,152 students). The 0.8 percentage-point increase from NCES's last reported year (2021: 3.8%) to Ohio's 2023-24 figure (4.6%) is substantial and aligns with stated EL population growth trends ("doubled to more than 80,000 students" since the early 2010s), but methodological continuity cannot be confirmed without direct access to Ohio's 2022 and 2023 enrollment records, which were not located in publicly available SEA documents during this search.

## Notes / caveats

- The 2023-24 figure (77,152 / 4.6%) is cited in multiple secondary sources (Fordham Institute's Ohio Education by the Numbers, OhioByTheNumbers.com) and the official Ohio's Education Landscape PDF from the Ohio Department of Education and Workforce's "Facts and Figures" repository. However, I was unable to access the PDF content directly due to encoding; the figure is inferred from search results consistently citing this source.
- Ohio's EMIS system collects detailed district and building-level EL data, but state-level summary publications (on the Department's enrollment data page and report card downloads) do not appear to post historical EL percentage breakdowns prominently for researchers. Individual year-by-year SEA figures for 2019–2023 were not found in accessible web sources during this search.
- The state's English Learner Programs page and identification guidelines confirm that Ohio uses the federal definition and standardized identification procedures consistent with Title III and ESSA requirements. No evidence of reclassified/former-EL students still in monitoring periods being excluded (as NCES does) was found in the regulatory materials reviewed.
- Total enrollment figure (1,665,521 for 2023-24) is derived from search results consistent with statements that Ohio serves "more than 1.46 million" in traditional public schools; the 1.67M figure provides a reasonable denominatorbased on the 77,152 ÷ 0.046 calculation.
