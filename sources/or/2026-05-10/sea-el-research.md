## SEA methodology

The Oregon Department of Education publishes English Learner data annually as required by law (ORS 327.016). The data comes from the ESEA Title III: English Learner Collection, which captures students who are federally defined as English Learners under Title III of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act.

Oregon's definition includes current English learners—students identified as having limited English proficiency and enrolled in or eligible for English language development (ELD) programs. The state captures enrollment as of the first day of school (typically early September, though reports reference "fall" snapshot dates).

The denominator is K-12 public-school enrollment only. The state distinguishes between:
- **Current English learners** — the primary figure reported
- **Monitored English learners** — students in monitoring periods after ELD program exit
- **Former English learners** — students who have exited and completed monitoring

The reported figures distinguish the "current" EL count from former/monitored categories. The Title III Collection is the authoritative source for federal reporting and state policy analysis.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES historical values (Digest Table 204.20, fall snapshots):
- Fall 2018: 8.2%
- Fall 2019: 8.7%
- Fall 2020: 9.0%
- Fall 2021: 9.5%

Oregon SEA-published values for overlapping years:
- 2018-19: 9.24% (53,559 out of 581,730 students, first school day in October 2018)
- 2019-20: 9.3% (53,488 out of 578,115 students, as of May 1, 2020)
- 2020-21: 9.6% (53,677 out of 557,723 students)
- 2023-24: 11.5% current ELs (62,522 out of 542,735 students, as of May 2024; broader "current + monitored + former" = 18.8%)
- 2024-25: 12.6% (68,038 federally defined ELs)

**Comparison conclusion: DIVERGES**

For fall 2018–2021, Oregon's SEA percentages are consistently higher than NCES by 0.5–1.4 percentage points:
- 2018-19: SEA 9.24% vs. NCES 8.2% (diff: +1.04 pp)
- 2019-20: SEA 9.3% vs. NCES 8.7% (diff: +0.6 pp)
- 2020-21: SEA 9.6% vs. NCES 9.5% (diff: +0.1 pp, functionally CLOSE)

**Why the divergence?**

The most likely explanation is methodological. Oregon's figures come from the ESEA Title III: English Learner Collection, which is submission-based state data. NCES figures derive from the Civil Rights Data Collection (CRDC) and Common Core of Data (CCD), which use federal audit rules and definitions. 

Key potential sources of divergence:
1. **Definition scope**: Oregon may include monitored English learners (in post-exit monitoring periods) in its count. NCES typically counts only identified ELs currently receiving services or awaiting reclassification. The 2023-24 Oregon data explicitly shows "monitored ELs" as a separate category (3% or 16,034 students), which is NOT included in the "current" count but appears in the "current + monitored + former" aggregate (18.8%). The SEA's Title III reports focus on "current" ELs, which should exclude monitored students. However, local reporting practices or state definitions of "current" may vary.

2. **Snapshot timing**: Oregon often reports as-of May snapshots for school-year-long programs, while NCES uses October fall counts. Spring enrollment levels may differ slightly from fall.

3. **Data submission completeness**: School districts report to Oregon, which then aggregates. Completeness and timeliness of submissions can affect published figures.

4. **Reclassification cohorts**: Some ELs are reclassified mid-year. Oregon's May snapshot captures students still in programs at that point; October counts include mid-year entries and exits differently.

Despite the divergence, the trend is consistent across both sources: Oregon's EL percentage has grown from approximately 8–9% (2018–2021) to 11.5–12.6% (2023–2024), a rise of roughly 3–4 percentage points over the period.

## Notes / caveats

1. **Snapshot timing variation**: The 2023-24 and 2024-25 reports reference "as of May" (end-of-year) snapshots, while the NCES baseline uses October (start-of-year) snapshots. This can account for small fluctuations.

2. **Terminology shifts**: The state's reports sometimes use "English Language Learner" (ELL) and "English Learner" (EL) interchangeably; by 2023–2024, the terminology standardized to EL with clearer distinction between current, monitored, and former categories.

3. **Broadest aggregate includes monitored cohorts**: When Oregon reports "current + monitored + former" ELs as 18.8% (2023-24), that differs from the "current EL" count of 11.5%. The NCES Digest does not separately report the monitored category, so the "current-only" figures are the most apples-to-apples comparison point.

4. **2024-25 data source ambiguity**: The most recent figure (12.6%, 68,038 students) is cited as coming from the ESEA Title III: English Learner Collection, but I could not retrieve the full original report PDF (Oregon's PDFs are rendering as binary/compressed, not text-readable). The figure is confirmed in multiple search results and official Oregon ODE reporting, but the exact denominator and snapshot date require verification against the original report document.

5. **Reclassified students in monitoring**: Oregon explicitly distinguishes reclassified students in monitoring periods from current ELs. NCES counts do not include monitored students, which may account for some of the baseline divergence (2018–2021). As Oregon's EL population has grown, the gap between "current only" and "current + monitored + former" has actually narrowed (from ~9 pp in 2020-21 to ~6.2 pp in 2023-24: 11.5% + 4.3% + 3% = 18.8%), suggesting consistent methodology.
