# Rhode Island — SEA EL enrollment research (2026-05-10)

## Most recent SEA-published EL figures

- **School year:** 2023-24
- **Snapshot date:** 2023-10-01
- **EL (or equivalent) count:** ~18,381
- **Total enrollment (denominator):** 136,154
- **EL percent:** 13.5%
- **Source label:** Rhode Island Department of Education (RIDE) Strategy for Improving Outcomes for Multilingual Learners (March 2024)
- **Source URL:** https://ride.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur806/files/2024-03/Encl5a_MLL.pdf
- **Local terminology:** "Multilingual Learner" (MLL); Rhode Island officially uses "Multilingual Learner" terminology, though "English Learner" (EL) is used in regulations and federal reporting. Historically used "English Language Learner" (ELL).

## SEA methodology

Rhode Island identifies students as Multilingual Learners (MLLs) using a Home Language Survey (HLS) administered by the Rhode Island Department of Elementary and Secondary Education when students first register or re-enroll in an LEA. If the home language is other than English, students are referred for an English Language Screener administered by qualified assessors. The definition under 200-RICR-20-30-3 states that an MLL is "a student whose home language is other than English and who has not yet attained enough proficiency in English to allow them to fully access and meaningfully engage in all of the school's curricular and extracurricular programs and services."

The identification methodology includes assessment of English language proficiency across six proficiency levels (Entering, Emerging, Developing, Expanding, Bridging, and Reaching) using WIDA-endorsed English language development standards. When available, literacy assessments in the student's home language are also administered.

The 13.5% figure comes from comparison data in a March 2024 RIDE document noting growth "from 9.9% of students to 13.5% of students" over a 5-year period, meaning this represents data as of the 2023-24 school year with an October 1, 2023 snapshot date. Total enrollment on October 1, 2023, per Rhode Island KIDS COUNT was 136,154 K-12 public school students.

## Comparison to NCES

NCES Digest Table 204.20 provides historical data for Rhode Island:

| Year | NCES % | NCES Count | Notes |
|------|--------|-----------|-------|
| Fall 2018 | 9.8% | 14,126 | Baseline |
| Fall 2019 | 11.9% | 17,116 | +2.1 pp |
| Fall 2020 | 12.0% | 16,684 | +0.1 pp |
| Fall 2021 | 12.5% | 17,289 | +0.5 pp |

**Comparison to SEA figure:**
- NCES Fall 2021: 12.5% (17,289 students)
- RIDE 2023-24 (Oct 2023): 13.5% (~18,381 students)

**Conclusion:** DIVERGES slightly. The Rhode Island RIDE figure of 13.5% in 2023-24 is 1.0 percentage points higher than the NCES Fall 2021 baseline of 12.5%. This represents continued growth in the MLL population and is consistent with documented trends. RIDE documents confirm a 5-year growth trajectory (9.9% → 13.5%), which places 2021's 12.5% within the documented growth curve. The divergence appears methodologically continuous — both are measuring identified students in K-12 public schools on October 1 snapshot dates — but the RIDE figure is 1 percentage point higher, reflecting approximately 18 months of enrollment growth (Oct 2021 to Oct 2023) and population shifts in Rhode Island.

## Notes / caveats

1. **Exact October 2023 enrollment count assumption:** The 13.5% figure from the March 2024 RIDE document was calculated by applying the stated 5-year growth trajectory (9.9% → 13.5%) to the documented October 1, 2023 total enrollment of 136,154. The document states growth "from 9.9% of students to 13.5% of students" without specifying the exact snapshot date, but context indicates this is October 2023 data in the 2023-24 school year. The estimated MLL count of ~18,381 (136,154 × 0.135) is derived from this application.

2. **No newer data found for FY 2024-25 snapshot.** While multiple RIDE documents reference October 2024 data collection, the published figures available as of May 10, 2026 are October 2023 snapshots from the 2023-24 school year (13.5%). No finalized October 2024 figures were accessible.

3. **Terminology distinction:** Rhode Island's shift from "English Language Learner" (ELL) to "Multilingual Learner" (MLL) occurred in regulatory/policy documents but both terms refer to the same federally-tracked population. The NCES historical data and RIDE current data are directly comparable.

4. **Monitoring vs. classified distinction:** RIDE regulations distinguish between actively-identified MLLs requiring services and former MLLs in monitoring status. The 13.5% figure reflects only those classified as active MLLs, not monitoring-only students. This aligns with NCES methodology, which also counts only identified ELs.

5. **Assessment test burden note:** RIDE data confirm that in the last six years (2018–2024), the number of MLLs tested increased by 38% (an increase of 5,394 students), which exceeds the percentage growth in population classification itself, suggesting improved testing/participation rather than classification drift alone.
