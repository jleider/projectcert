## SEA methodology

**Definition:** An English Learner (EL) is a student with a Non-English Language Background (NELB) who qualifies for English as a Second Language (ESL) services based on an English Language Proficiency screener approved by the Department of Education. NELB students are identified via home language survey (HLS); all potential NELB students must be screened within 30 days of enrollment.

**Screener:** Tennessee uses only the WIDA Screener for Kindergarten and WIDA Screener for grades 1-12 as approved screening instruments.

**Denominator:** K-12 public schools (based on Tennessee Department of Education membership files, which capture enrollment as of October 1 of the school year).

**Snapshot date:** October 1 of the applicable school year (Tennessee enrollment files are dated October 1, aligning with NCES CCD standards).

**Regulatory basis:** Tennessee State Board of Education Rule 0520-01-19 (English as a Second Language Programs Rule).

## Notes / caveats

1. **Recent enrollment counts lack denominator:** The SEA states "exceeded 83,000" English learners in 2024-25 but does not publish a corresponding total K-12 enrollment figure for the same year. The 6.6% figure for 2022-23 comes from secondary sources (likely research institutions or news summaries aggregating district-level data), not a direct SEA statistical publication.

2. **Data currency lag:** As of 2026-05-10, the most recent SEA enrollment figures are from 2024-25. The 2024-25 school year has ended, but the Tennessee Department of Education has not yet released a comprehensive state-level statistical report or data download with both EL count and total enrollment for that year.

3. **Program enrollment vs. classification:** Tennessee's identification rule (Rule 0520-01-19) requires screening all NELB students, so the EL count reflects *classification* (students who meet the definition and are eligible for ESL services), not program enrollment. This is methodologically sound and aligns with NCES practice.

4. **Lack of SEA historical summary:** Unlike some states, Tennessee does not appear to maintain a published multi-year EL enrollment table (e.g., a "Fact Sheet" or "Dashboard" widget). Researchers and advocates cite historical figures, but these are reconstructed from district-level downloads or ED-Facts submissions. The Tennessee Department of Education Data Downloads & Requests page and State Report Card exist but do not appear to surface state-level EL enrollment trends in an easy-to-cite summary.

5. **No reclassified-EL disclosure:** The rule does not explicitly address whether Tennessee's EL count includes reclassified (former-EL, monitoring-period) students. NCES does not include reclassified students in its table. If Tennessee's count differs on this point, it would explain any divergence, but the SEA has not published clarification.

6. **Verification status:** The most recent 2024-25 data is fresh (confirmed as current on the SEA's main English Learners page), but the absence of a directly-published statistical report, trend table, or methodology note prevents confident verification. The figure is stated but not substantiated with a link to a downloadable data file or official statistical publication.
