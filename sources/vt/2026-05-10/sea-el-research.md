## SEA methodology

Vermont identifies English Learners using a multi-step process outlined in statute:

1. **Home Language Survey**: Administered to all kindergarten students and all students new to Vermont K-12 schools (public or public/private academies). If a language other than English is indicated, the student proceeds to screening.

2. **Informal Interview**: A family interview is conducted to determine how the non-English language is used at home and whether it affects the student's ability to meaningfully access the standard curriculum in English.

3. **Formal Screener**: Students meeting screening criteria are assessed using the WIDA Screener (required assessment tool).

4. **Ongoing Monitoring**: All students identified as English learners participate annually in the state's English language proficiency assessment (WIDA ACCESS, ACCESS for Kindergarten, or Alternate ACCESS) to monitor progress in acquiring academic English across four domains: reading, writing, listening, and speaking.

**Definition**: Per Vermont statute and federal guidance (20 U.S.C. § 7801), English Learners include all students ages 3–21 identified through the home language survey and screening process whose primary language is not English and who lack sufficient proficiency in English to meaningfully access instruction.

**Denominator**: K-12 public school enrollment (October 1st snapshot). Vermont's data does not include pre-K or post-secondary programs in the K-12 denominator, though Act 128 (2022) expanded identification criteria to include age 3+.

**Snapshot date**: October 1st, consistent with NCES methodology (CCD Common Core of Data).

Vermont submits English Learner data to the federal government through EDFacts for ESSA accountability reporting. Most Vermont school districts do not qualify for Title III funding because they educate fewer EL students; however, supervisory unions in "Valley school districts" that educate approximately 70% of the state's EL students do qualify for Title III funding.

## Notes / caveats

1. **Most recent figure uncertainty**: The Vermont Agency of Education does not appear to publish a single, definitive statewide EL enrollment percentage in a static, regularly-cited report. The "about 2%" figure for 2022-23 comes from the Legislative Joint Fiscal Office report on EL education costs, not directly from the agency's annual data publications. This report is focused on cost analysis rather than routine enrollment reporting.

2. **Burlington enrollment data source**: The 510 EL student figure for Burlington School District (2023 school year, representing ~27% of the state's EL population) comes from the same LJFO report and implies a statewide total of approximately 1,889 EL students. This is a calculated inference, not a directly cited statewide enrollment total.

3. **Dashboard access limitation**: The Vermont Education Dashboard: Student Characteristics contains historical data and allows selection of teaching year, but the interactive nature of the system means specific percentages are not indexed or accessible via web search. A researcher with direct dashboard access or someone contacting the Vermont Agency of Education data team would be able to extract historical comparisons with NCES.

4. **Title III funding note**: Vermont's EL students are concentrated in a few supervisory unions. Burlington School District, which educates ~27% of the state's EL population, is the primary Title III recipient, suggesting potential regional variation in EL concentration that may differ from the national pattern.

5. **Terminology consistency**: Vermont uses "English Learner," "English Language Learner," and "Multilingual Learner" interchangeably in agency publications, consistent with contemporary federal naming conventions.

6. **No reclassification caveats identified**: The research did not identify specific Vermont policy documents addressing whether the state's count includes reclassified/former-EL students still in a monitoring period (a potential methodological difference from NCES). The home language survey and WIDA screener process does not clearly distinguish between newly identified and monitored former-ELs in available public descriptions, so this remains unverified.
